Bigger budgets gross more, but many films sit below break-even

Each dot is a film. Films under the dashed line earned less worldwide than they cost to make, before marketing, which this data does not include. Both axes are log scales.

Median return by genre

Genres with at least 30 films in the selected era. A film counts in each of its genres. The figure at the end of each bar is that genre's median budget: genres made cheaply can post high ratios on small budgets, so read the two together.

Median gross by release month

Summer and the winter holidays lead. Studios save their biggest films for these windows, so this mixes timing with which films get released when.

Median return by decade

All years, decades with at least 30 films. Older decades look better partly because old films stay in the data only if people still rate them today.

Released films with both budget and revenue known, compared with those missing either. Because they differ this much, missing budgets were not filled in, and every figure above describes well-documented films only.

Definitions and data
ROI
Worldwide gross divided by production budget. 1 means the film earned back exactly its budget.
Out-grossed budget
ROI above 1. The true break-even is higher once marketing and the cinemas' share of ticket sales are counted.
Constant 2025 dollars
Budgets and grosses adjusted with the US consumer price index (CPI-U), so decades are comparable. ROI is unaffected.
Source
The Movie Database (TMDB), collected through its public API, cleaned in the IT 462 project notebooks. This product uses the TMDB API but is not endorsed or certified by TMDB.
Caution
All figures are associations in observational data, not causes. Big budgets travel with big marketing campaigns, and marketing spend is not in the data.
